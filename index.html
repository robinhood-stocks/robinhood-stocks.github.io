Robinhood Stocks.

Robinhood Stocks is the equity trading side of the Robinhood app, letting account holders buy and sell shares of publicly traded companies and exchange-traded funds without paying a per-trade commission. The product grew out of a mobile-first, no-minimum approach to investing, pairing a simple order screen with real-time quotes, fractional share purchases, and order types that mirror what older full-service brokers offer. Robinhood Stocks sits in the same account as options, crypto, and cash management, so one login and one funded balance move across asset classes without a separate sign-up.

How Robinhood Stocks Trading Works

Robinhood Stocks is Robinhood's commission-free brokerage service for buying and selling shares, ETFs, and other listed securities directly from a mobile or web account. When a user places an order, it is routed to a market maker or exchange for execution, and the resulting shares or cash settle into the account without the flat per-trade fee that brokers historically charged. The company's background and growth from a mobile-only startup into a public brokerage is documented on its Wikipedia entry, which covers the regulatory history alongside the product itself. Funding comes instead from interest on uninvested cash, a premium subscription tier, and the order-routing arrangements described later in this guide. The interface itself is built around a single search bar and a stock detail page, so finding a company, reading its chart, and placing an order all happen within the same short flow rather than across separate research and trading tools.

The Commission-Free Model

Before Robinhood's launch, most brokers charged several dollars per trade; the zero-commission structure, explained in general terms by Investopedia, pushed much of the brokerage industry to match it within a few years. The model works because revenue comes from sources other than the trade ticket itself, which is why order routing and interest income matter as much to the business as the number of trades placed.

Account Funding and Settlement

New accounts link a bank account for transfers, and many deposits become usable for trading before they fully clear, subject to limits that scale with account history. Stock trades settle on a T+1 basis across the industry, so cash from a sold position becomes available the next business day rather than the moment the sale executes.

Robinhood Stocks Order Types and Fractional Shares

Placing a trade on Robinhood Stocks means choosing both a quantity and an order type, and the two work together differently depending on how a stock is priced and how much of it a user wants to hold. A standard market order fills at the best available price immediately, while a limit order only fills at a price the user sets or better, giving up speed for price control. Stop and stop-limit orders trigger only once a stock crosses a chosen level, which is typically used to manage an existing position rather than to open a new one.

Fractional Share Purchases

Robinhood Stocks allows orders in dollar amounts rather than whole shares, so a stock trading at several hundred dollars can be bought in a fraction as small as a small percentage of one share. This removes the need to buy a full share of an expensive company and lets a fixed dollar amount be spread across several stocks at once.

Reading Quotes Before an Order

Every order screen shows a live bid, ask, and last-traded price sourced from the exchanges, and checking a broader quote feed such as Nasdaq's market activity data before placing a limit order helps confirm where a stock is actually trading relative to the price typed into the order ticket.

Payment for Order Flow and Robinhood Stocks Execution

Robinhood Stocks does not route most orders directly to a stock exchange. Instead, orders are sent to market makers who pay the brokerage for the right to execute them, an arrangement known as payment for order flow and described at length on Wikipedia's entry on the practice. The market maker profits from the small spread between the buy and sell price, and in exchange the brokerage can offer trading without a commission.

Execution Quality and Price Improvement

Regulators require brokers to disclose routing practices and execution statistics, and a trade can still receive a price slightly better than the quoted one even when it is routed to a market maker rather than an exchange, a concept covered in the investor-education material at Investor.gov. Comparing a filled price against the quote at the moment of the order is the simplest way to judge execution quality on any individual trade.

Extended Hours Trading on Robinhood Stocks

Standard exchange hours run from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern, but Robinhood Stocks also supports trading outside that window on many listed securities. A pre-market session opens several hours before the regular bell, and an after-hours session continues for a few hours once it closes, letting account holders react to news released outside normal trading hours. A smaller set of stocks on the platform supports trading across a wider, near around-the-clock window on top of the standard pre-market and after-hours sessions. Earnings reports, economic data releases, and other scheduled news often land before the opening bell or after the closing bell, which is part of why extended-hours volume tends to cluster around those specific windows rather than spreading evenly across the full session.

Why Extended Hours Behave Differently

Fewer participants are active outside standard hours, so the gap between the buy and sell price tends to be wider and prices can move more sharply on smaller trading volume. Limit orders are commonly used during these sessions rather than market orders, since a market order placed when few other orders are present can fill at a price further from the last standard-hours trade than expected.

Robinhood Stocks Account Types

Robinhood Stocks is offered through a standard brokerage account, and the same login also supports retirement accounts and a margin-enabled tier for users who qualify. Robinhood Stocks vs Robinhood Crypto

Robinhood Stocks and Robinhood's crypto offering live in the same app but work on different infrastructure. Stock orders flow through the equity market structure described above, settle on the T+1 cycle, and are covered by standard securities protections for cash and shares held at the brokerage. Crypto orders instead settle on their underlying blockchains and are held through a separate crypto entity, a distinction the company's own disclosures and its company history page both describe when outlining how the product line expanded beyond equities. A trader who holds both a stock position and a crypto position in Robinhood is using two separate systems under one shared login, and the concepts that apply to one, such as payment for order flow, do not automatically carry over to the other in the same form, as general reference material on brokerage asset classes explains. Trading hours are another practical difference: equity trading is bound to the standard and extended sessions described above, while crypto markets trade continuously since there is no exchange open-and-close cycle underneath them.

Getting Started with Robinhood Stocks

Opening and funding an account follows the same basic sequence most new users go through before placing a first order on Robinhood Stocks.
  • Open an account and complete identity verification required of every regulated brokerage.
  • Link a bank account and transfer the amount intended for investing.
  • Search for a stock or ETF and review its quote, chart, and recent price history.
  • Choose an order type — market, limit, or stop — and enter a share count or dollar amount.
  • Review the order summary, including any extended-hours or fractional-share details, before confirming.
  • Track filled orders and settled cash in the account activity section.

Questions & answers

Is there a minimum amount needed to start with Robinhood Stocks?
No. Accounts can be opened with no minimum deposit, and fractional share purchases allow a position to be opened with a small dollar amount rather than the price of a full share.
How does Robinhood Stocks make money without charging commissions?
Revenue comes from payment for order flow, interest earned on uninvested cash balances, and a premium subscription tier, rather than from a fee charged on each individual trade.
Can I trade stocks outside normal market hours?
Yes. Robinhood Stocks supports pre-market and after-hours sessions on many listed securities, and a smaller set of stocks supports an extended, near around-the-clock trading window.
What is the difference between a market order and a limit order?
A market order fills immediately at the best available price, while a limit order only fills at a chosen price or better, trading speed for price control.
How long does it take for stock trades to settle?
Robinhood Stocks follows the industry-wide T+1 settlement cycle, so cash from a sold position becomes available the next business day.
Are Robinhood Stocks and Robinhood Crypto the same product?
No. They share one app and login but run on different infrastructure: stock trades settle through the equity market structure, while crypto trades settle on blockchains and are held through a separate entity.
What does payment for order flow mean for a stock trade?
It means the order is routed to a market maker that pays the brokerage for the right to execute it, rather than being sent directly to an exchange, which is part of how the zero-commission model is funded.
